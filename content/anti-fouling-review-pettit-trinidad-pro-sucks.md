<p>In October 2020 we decided to do a bottom job before leaving Grenada. Last time we did one was 1 year before in Trinidad. This time around we used <strong>Pettit Trinidad Pro.</strong></p>
<p>We chose this antifouling because a) it's environmentally friendly (it does not contain TBT) and b) because it has a quite high percentage of copper.</p>
<img class="aligncenter wp-image-7490" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/trinidad-pro-400x500-1.png" alt="Pettit Trinidad Pro" width="186" height="233" />
<p>From Pettit Trinidad Pro Product Data Sheet:</p>
<p><em>"Trinidad Pro’s high copper load is paired with a specially formulated algaecide to create an antifoulant with unprecedented resistance to barnacles, algae, slime, and other marine and fresh-water fouling organisms. Left in the water, it will provide years of dependable service. Trinidad Pro is the only hard paint with the power of dual biocides available for the professional applicator."</em></p>
<img class="aligncenter wp-image-7486" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/trinidadpro.jpg" alt="Pettit Trinidad Pro" width="583" height="228" />
<p>The anti-fouling did a great job until Puerto Rico.  Just a few small barnacles, no slime.  But then, after 4 weeks at anchor in San Juan, this was the result:</p>
<img class="size-full wp-image-7484" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/gopr3815.jpg" alt="Pettit Trinidad Pro anti-fouling" width="4000" height="3000" />
<img class="size-full wp-image-7482" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/gopr3810.jpg" alt="Pettit Trinidad Pro anti-fouling" width="4000" height="3000" />
<p>In two years, two continents and 12K+ nautical miles we've never seen such a thing.  Not even in Brazil, where water is very rich and where we left the boat for one month.  Or in Grenada, where we left the boat for 6 weeks.</p>
<p>It's very hard to get a serious opinion on what anti-fouling works best, because everyone say what they have it's the best.  Hard to find honest reviews online.</p>
<p>I would give Pettit Trinidad Pro a 2 stars rating.</p>
<p>How does Pettit Trinidad Pro compare to other anti-fouling we have used?</p>
<p>So far on Oroboro we have tried 3 different brands of anti-fouling, every year a different one.</p>
<p>Out of the factory, the boat come with<strong> Sea Hawk <span class="il">Bio</span> <span class="il">Cop</span> TF</strong>. It contains a low percentage of copper, just 36%.</p>
<img class="aligncenter wp-image-7489" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/sea-hawk-bio-cop-tf.jpg" alt="Sea Hawk Bio Cop TF" width="220" height="220" />
<p>From their Product Data Sheet:</p>
<p><em>"Biocop TF was developed as an alternative to tin-based antifouling paints to protect against the harshest marine environments and provide maximum protection. A new dual-biocide technology and revolutionary polymer binder system “PL3” produces an engineered biocide that leaves no harmful effects on the environment. Biocop TF is considered the top performer among antifouling paints and is safe in salt or fresh water on boats of all sizes."</em></p>
<img class="aligncenter wp-image-7492" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/sea-hawk-bio-cop-tf-data-sheet-1.jpg" alt="Sea-Hawk-Bio-Cop-TF-DAta-Sheet" width="335" height="144" />
<p>This antifouling was very good and lasted us more than a year.  The boat spent 3 months in Cape Grace marina in Cape Town, then one month in a marina in Salvador the Baia. When we arrived in Trinidad around November, we did a haul out and a bottom job.   I would give it a 4 stars rating.</p>
<p>In Trinidad we were recommended to use <strong><span class="il">International</span> <span class="il">Interspeed</span> 6400NA</strong>.</p>
<img class="aligncenter wp-image-7537" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/img_0521.jpg" width="285" height="249" />
<p>From their Product Data Sheet:</p>
<p><em>"A high performance, TBT free, polishing antifouling. Enhanced biocide release mechanism. Prevents coating build -up. At subsequent drydockings, it is only necessary to top up the system. Low VOC."</em></p>
<img class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-7499" src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/interspeed.jpg" alt="Interspeed" width="1874" height="514" />
<p>We got a very good deal and so we gave it a try.  It was different from the previous anti-fouling, it was a semi-hard one.</p>
<p>From Grenada to Barbuda and back, it did not show any barnacles, but a lot of slime.</p>
<p>I would give it a 3 stars rating.</p>
<p>Lesson learned:<br>
<ol><br>
 	<li>We prefer hard anti-fouling over soft or semi-hard ones. Scraping a hard anti-fouling is better that scraping a soft one, because you don't take off the anti-fouling together with the barnacles.</li><br>
 	<li>We still prefer TBT free environmentally friendly anti-fouling.  We know people who applied Sea Hawk Island 44 the same time we did and sailed in the same places (including spending 4 weeks in San Juan) and their bottom is equally bad. So contrary to popular belief, it's not TBT that makes your anti-fouling work better. And TBT is not good for the environment either.</li><br>
 	<li>The quantity of copper does not seem to make things any better. The Sea Hawk <span class="il">Bio</span> <span class="il">Cop</span> TF contained less copper than Pettit Trinidad Pro but worked better.</li><br>
</ol><br>
At the end of the day, we think we should have sticked to <strong>Sea Hawk <span class="il">Bio</span> <span class="il">Cop</span> TF.  </strong>It is 30% cheaper than Pettit Trinidad Pro and we got better results.</p>
<p>Maybe next year we'll try Copper Coat or we'll go back to Sea Hawk <span class="il">Bio</span> <span class="il">Cop</span> TF.</p>
