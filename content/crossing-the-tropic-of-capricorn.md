<p>We crossed the Tropic of Capricorn at latitude&nbsp;23°26′ exactly on the Winter Solstice.&nbsp; What are the chances!&nbsp; This is the time of the year&nbsp;when the Sun reaches its zenith at this latitude.&nbsp; For someone who studied Celestial Navigation this is a very interesting phenomenon. We are officially in the tropics, even if the temperature says otherwise...</p>
<h3>Spencer Bay</h3>After leaving Hottentot Bay we sailed close to the coast up to Spencer Bay, where we were hoping we could have gone to shore to visit the famous Otavi shipwreck.&nbsp;&nbsp;The Otavi&nbsp;was a steamer with a cargo of Guano that ran aground in 1945. Guano was found in abundance on Mercury island, right in Spencer Bay.
<p>Same as the day before in Hottentoy Bay, by the time we arrived the wind picked up from 20 to 40 knots.&nbsp; We spent the night at anchor in hauling winds.&nbsp; The next day the wind was gone, but the swell left behind was too large for our little dinghy to attempt a visit on shore.</p>
<p>So we carried on to Walvis Bay.&nbsp; The coast was just spectacular.<br>

<h3 id="firstHeading" class="firstHeading" lang="en">Swakopmund</h3><br>
<p id="firstHeading" class="firstHeading" lang="en">The following day we visited Swakopmund, a very nice town north of Walvis Bay,&nbsp;an example of German&nbsp;colonial&nbsp;architecture. It was founded in 1892 as the main&nbsp;harbour&nbsp;for&nbsp;German South West Africa, and a small part of its population is still German-speaking today.</p><br>
<p lang="en"></p><br>
<p lang="en"><img src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/img_1856.jpg" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1542" alt="Swakopmund, Namibia" width="4032" height="3024"></p></p>
<h3 lang="en">Dune #7</h3>Dune #7 takes its name because it is the seventh dune one encounters after crossing the river Tsauchab.&nbsp; It is some 400 meters high.&nbsp; Many South African who served in the infantry may remember this dune because there was an outpost of the army right here and they had to practice on this dune.
<img src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/img_1871.jpg" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1538" alt="Dune number 7, Namibia" width="3874" height="2906">
<h3>Sandwhich Bay</h3>Sandwich Harbour, part of the Namib Naukluft Park, is a place many have heard of but very few have ever visited. The lagoon, salt pans and the bird sanctuary, which form the Walvis Bay Wetlands are unique.
<p>Giant sand dunes run straight into the ocean, creating breathtaking sceneries and unique landscapes.</p>
<p>We had a very fun day exploring this huge National Park in a 4X4 vehicle.&nbsp; Here is a shot video of the experience:</p>
<p>The view of the ocean and the sand dunes was spectacular:</p>
<h3>Goanikontes Oasis</h3>This is a lunar landscape, with a beautiful oasis in the middle of nowhere.
<img src="https://pub-7f7d07c430fd4c3eb11a4e6eae938ce3.r2.dev/img_1851.jpg" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1540" alt="Goanikontes Oasis, Namibia" width="4032" height="3024">
<h3>New Year's Eve</h3>We decided to set sails for St. Helena on January 1st 2019. The first day of the new year.&nbsp; The decision was based on weather forecasts.&nbsp; We've had some unfavorable north westerly winds in the last few days, but according to the forecasts on Jan 1st the wind should be from the south again.
<p>Big dilemma on board Oroboro: when is going to be midnight for us so that we can start the new year celebration? It's complicated:</p>
<p>We are at 14 degrees 28 minutes East of longitude here in Walvis Bay. So technically we should be UTC+1.</p>
<p>However, Namibia keeps South Africa time that is UTC+2 (which is technically wrong also).<br>
<div class="text_exposed_show"></p>
<p>So to resolve the issue, as Captain of the Ship I issued an Executive Order declaring it is midnight right now at sundown, and the bottle of champagne was opened to keep with the tradition.</p>
<p>Happy New Year everybody!</p>
