# Episode 3 — St. Helena: Napoleon's Island
### Language: English
### Format: Interview (Host + Francesco)
### Estimated duration: ~6 minutes

---

**HOST:** Welcome back to Sailing Oroboro. Last episode we met the boat — the Leopard 40 that Francesco and Yuka would call home for years. At the end of that episode, December 15th, 2018 — they cast off the lines in Cape Town and headed into the Atlantic. Francesco... what does that moment feel like? When the dock actually disappears behind you?

**FRANCESCO:** It's... hard to describe. We'd been preparing for years. And then suddenly it's real. The marina shrinks behind you. Cape Town, Table Mountain... getting smaller. And you realize — there's no going back. Not right now. The ocean is ahead, and that's it.

**HOST:** Where were you headed?

**FRANCESCO:** St. Helena. A tiny volcanic island in the middle of the South Atlantic. About 1,200 miles from any continent. It's famous for one thing: Napoleon was exiled there after Waterloo. The British picked it specifically because there was nowhere to escape TO.

**HOST:** How long was the passage?

**FRANCESCO:** Twenty-five days. Just the two of us. No other boats in sight for days at a time. No land. No internet. Just the boat, the wind, and the sea.

**HOST:** Were you scared?

**FRANCESCO:** Before we left — yes. I'd been thinking about that passage for months. But once we were actually out there... something shifted. The fear goes away. What replaces it is this extraordinary stillness. No news. No decisions except the next watch change. Just the ocean.

**HOST:** What do you remember most from those twenty-five days?

**FRANCESCO:** The dolphins. Almost every day, a pod would find us. One morning they swam with us for two hours — riding the bow wave, crossing under the hulls. Yuka and I sat on the trampoline at the front of the boat and watched them in silence. I don't think either of us said a word for a long time.

**HOST:** And then — St. Helena. What's the arrival like?

**FRANCESCO:** You see it from miles away — this dark volcanic shape rising out of the ocean. Just... rock. No beach. Cliffs straight up from the water. We picked up our mooring buoy on January 9th. And I remember thinking: we've just crossed an ocean. We actually did it.

**HOST:** What IS St. Helena? Most people have no idea.

**FRANCESCO:** It's a British territory — about 4,000 people, stuck in the middle of nowhere. For centuries the only way to reach it was by sea. An airport was built a few years before we arrived... but the tourists never came. So it's completely unspoiled. Which is a gift, honestly.

**HOST:** Let's talk about Napoleon.

**FRANCESCO:** So after he escaped from his first exile — on the island of Elba, off Italy — the British needed somewhere he absolutely could NOT escape from. St. Helena was the answer. It's 1,200 miles from the nearest continent in every direction. He spent the last six years of his life there. We visited Longwood House... his home. His bedroom. The room where he died. It's sobering. The contrast between who he was — emperor of half of Europe — and where he ended up... on a humid little island in the middle of the ocean... is striking.

**HOST:** Did they bury him there?

**FRANCESCO:** Yes. In a valley called Sane Valley. A simple stone slab, iron railings, very quiet. His remains were eventually moved to Paris... but the original tomb is still there. We stood at it and just... took it in. There's something about standing in a place where history actually happened.

**HOST:** What else is worth seeing?

**FRANCESCO:** Jacob's Ladder — 699 steps cut straight into a volcanic cliff. Nearly vertical. The fastest recorded ascent is five minutes and sixteen seconds. We were NOT attempting that record.

**HOST:** And Jonathan?

**FRANCESCO:** Jonathan is the reason people gasp when you tell them about St. Helena. He's a giant tortoise — born around 1832. He was already FULLY GROWN when he arrived on the island in 1882. He's been photographed since 1886. And he's STILL ALIVE. He grazes in the grounds of the Governor's residence. You walk up to the fence and there he is... just eating... completely indifferent to you. The oldest known living land animal on Earth. And you can just... stand there and look at him.

**HOST:** What does it feel like — standing next to something that old?

**FRANCESCO:** Humbling. He was already old when Napoleon was there. He was alive for the entire twentieth century — both World Wars, the moon landing, all of it. And there he is. Eating grass. I found it genuinely moving.

**HOST:** Any practical advice for sailors stopping there?

**FRANCESCO:** Bring British pounds — no ATMs in town. Don't expect good provisioning — fresh vegetables are nearly impossible to find. And budget a few days minimum. You need it to see everything. Oh, and Jacob's Ladder. Do it. Your legs will hate you, but the view from the top makes it worth it.

**HOST:** Next episode: we leave St. Helena and cross the equator for the first time — heading north into Brazil. The South Atlantic is behind us. This is Sailing Oroboro.
