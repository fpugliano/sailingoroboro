# Episode 1 — Quitting Our Jobs
### Language: English
### Format: Interview (Host + Francesco)
### Estimated duration: ~4 minutes

---

**HOST:** Welcome to Sailing Oroboro — a podcast about trading a desk for a deck. I'm your host, and today I'm talking with Francesco, who together with his partner Yuka left their Silicon Valley careers to sail the world on their catamaran, Oroboro. Francesco, let's start at the very beginning. You and Yuka had good jobs. What were you doing?

**FRANCESCO:** We were both in tech, in Silicon Valley. I was at eBay, Yuka was at LinkedIn. Good jobs, interesting work, smart colleagues. By most measures, life was going very well.

**HOST:** So what made you want to leave all that behind?

**FRANCESCO:** We had a dream — to sail the world. And I think most people have dreams like that, but they keep putting them off. There's always a reason to wait: the mortgage, the career, the health insurance. And one day we just decided: a dream stays a dream if you're too scared to chase it. And in the end, it's not the years in your life that count — it's the life in your years.

**HOST:** Was it scary?

**FRANCESCO:** Absolutely. We had sleepless nights. What about our friends? What if we need medical care on the other side of the world? How long would our savings last? What if no one hires us back after years out of the workforce? These weren't abstract worries — they were real, they kept us awake.

**HOST:** So how did you finally pull the trigger?

**FRANCESCO:** The boat gave us the deadline. Once we knew the exact date for the splash — when Oroboro would first hit the water — we had something concrete. We said: that's the date. We picked a time that worked for both of us and went to work and quit.

**HOST:** On the same day?

**FRANCESCO:** Same day, same hour. We work in the same industry — it's a small world — and we thought it was better that way. We went through the plan one last time over breakfast. And then we left home with one mission.

**HOST:** And how did it feel, when you actually did it?

**FRANCESCO:** Sad and relieved, at the same time. We gave generous notice, which helped — enough time to hand things over properly, to say goodbye to everyone. And there are a lot of people after years in an industry.

**HOST:** Was there a moment when it really hit you — that this was real?

**FRANCESCO:** The last day. A colleague asked me how it felt. I didn't know what to say. Then I got home, and Yuka said: "Congratulations!" I asked what for. She said: "You just graduated from eBay." That was exactly right. It felt exactly like graduating from college — ending something that had shaped you, and stepping into something completely unknown.

**HOST:** What came next?

**FRANCESCO:** Packing. Shipping boxes to Cape Town — because that's where Oroboro was being built, in South Africa. Saying goodbye to our house. And then the beginning of everything.

**HOST:** Francesco, thank you. This is the story of how it all started. Next episode: the boat itself — what is a Leopard 40, and why did you choose it? I'm your host, and this is Sailing Oroboro.
