# Episode 2 — The Boat
### Language: English
### Format: Interview (Host + Francesco)
### Estimated duration: ~5 minutes

---

**HOST:** Welcome back to Sailing Oroboro. Last episode, Francesco told us how he and Yuka quit their Silicon Valley jobs to sail the world. Today: the boat. What is a Leopard 40... and how do you even begin choosing something you're going to live on in the middle of the ocean? Francesco — how long did this decision take?

**FRANCESCO:** A few years. Seriously. It wasn't like buying a car. This was going to be our home... our life support system... on open oceans. So we took our time.

**HOST:** First question — monohull or catamaran?

**FRANCESCO:** That was actually the easiest part. I'd done all my training in San Francisco bay on monohulls — everything from basic keelboat up to coastal passage making. But we'd also chartered catamarans in the Caribbean several times. So we knew both.

**HOST:** And the catamaran won.

**FRANCESCO:** For us, yes. The analogy I use is motorcycles. A monohull is like a dirt bike — it can climb over anything, handle rough conditions, punch into waves. A catamaran is more like a street bike. Fast, smooth, comfortable... but it wants good conditions. And since we were planning to sail the trade winds — mostly downwind passages — the catamaran made sense.

**HOST:** What else tipped the scale?

**FRANCESCO:** Livability. We were going to be on this boat for YEARS. Two hulls means two cabins, a proper saloon, space to actually live. And redundancy — two engines, two rudders, two of almost everything. If one fails... you have the other.

**HOST:** So you knew you wanted a catamaran. Then what — which one?

**FRANCESCO:** We went to boat shows. Compared everything side by side. Our final three were Leopard, Lagoon, and Fountaine Pajot. We'd chartered Leopards before... liked the way they sailed, liked the build quality. South African boats — they build them tough.

**HOST:** And Yuka made the final call?

**FRANCESCO:** She did. She fell in love with the 2018 Leopard 40. Completely. And honestly... when she saw it, I understood why. It's 40 feet but it feels like a much bigger boat inside. The architects did something clever with the layout.

**HOST:** New or used?

**FRANCESCO:** New. Partly because of time — we both had full time jobs, we couldn't fly around the country to inspect used boats. But mainly... the 2018 Leopard 40 was brand new. To buy one used, we'd have waited years. And we'd already decided: this happens before 2020. No more delays.

**HOST:** When did you first actually see Oroboro — YOUR boat?

**FRANCESCO:** Cape Town. Cape Grace marina. We walked down the dock and... there she was. We were ecstatic. She looked MORE beautiful than we'd imagined. Which, after years of dreaming, is not a feeling you expect.

**HOST:** Was she ready to sail?

**FRANCESCO:** Not even close. We spent weeks outfitting her — watermaker, solar panels, battery systems, radar, cockpit enclosure. The list was enormous. But that time in Cape Town... it was special. Watching her become our home, piece by piece.

**HOST:** And then the first sail?

**FRANCESCO:** We did a shakedown run up to Langebaan — a beautiful sheltered lagoon about a hundred miles north of Cape Town. Turquoise water, flat anchorage. Perfect place to test everything we'd installed. And it all worked. More or less.

**HOST:** More or less?

**FRANCESCO:** There's always something. That's sailing. But nothing that stopped us. On December 15th, 2018... we cast off the lines at Cape Grace and headed north into the Atlantic. That was it. We were gone.

**HOST:** Next episode: the first passage — Cape Town to St. Helena, Napoleon's island, in the middle of the South Atlantic. This is Sailing Oroboro.
